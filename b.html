Revision - Text Analysis

The purpose

When listening to, reading or view a text, ask yourself:

  • What message did the creator(s) of the text want to express to the listener(s)?
  • Why did the creator of the text want to get this message out?
  • Does the text have something important to say?
  • Who would find it important?
  • What might compel a person to create the text? To consume the text?

The bias present

Some Types of Bias

  • Cultural bias: favouring a specific culture that the text creator is a part of
  • Confirmation bias: including selective information that confirms what the text creator already believes and omitting other ideas and views
  • *Sometimes, bias can take the form of favouring a group or viewpoint over another. This includes, but is not limited to race, religion, gender, sexual orientation, political affiliation
  • Bias is shaped by personal experiences and values. It is not always malicious.

The codes and conventions

Codes

  • Technical Codes (the ways we create meaning by how we record an image)
    • Camera shots
    • editing
    • audio/music
    • lighting
  • Symbolic Codes (the ways we create meaning by what we see in an image)
    • SETTING ⇨ time and place of a narrative. These can be small or large spaces that create atmosphere.
    • MISE EN SCENE ⇨ ‘everything within the frame’ of a media product and how objects included in this frame have been arranged. Consider the set design, costume, props, staging and composition.
    • COLOUR
  • Audio Codes (the ways we create meaning through the use of sound)
  • Written Codes (the ways we construct meaning through the use of text)

Conventions

Conventions are repeated ways of constructing media works, using codes that, over time, have become accepted by audiences. (e.g. a fade to black indicates time has passed; a scene of a car chase will include dramatic music) The meanings that are constructed by codes and conventions are culturally determined.
